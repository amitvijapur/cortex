## Inspiration without copying

Use inspiration sites to study patterns, not to select a ready-made aesthetic. Record what you are learning from each reference.

- [Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/)
- [Godly](https://godly.website/)
- [SiteInspire](https://www.siteinspire.com/)
- [Land-book](https://land-book.com/)
- [Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/)

For every reference, write down:

1. The exact quality you want to learn from.
2. The part that fits your product.
3. The part you should not copy.
4. How the idea will change when applied to your own content.

## Suggested reading

- Robert Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*
- Josef Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*
- Ellen Lupton, *Thinking with Type*
- Don Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*
- Steve Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*
- Alla Kholmatova, *Design Systems*
- Refactoring UI by Adam Wathan and Steve Schoger

Books and galleries sharpen judgment, but the final design still needs to be grounded in the product's own subject matter.
